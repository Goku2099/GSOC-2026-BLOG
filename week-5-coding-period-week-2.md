## My First Week of GSoC Coding Period
The first week of the GSoC coding period was mainly focused on implementing one of the most important Spark-related functionalities of my project.
Before writing any code, I created an end-to-end workflow document to clearly explain the implementation approach. After discussing it with my mentors and getting a green signal, I moved on to the implementation phase.
A significant portion of this week was spent working with Docker, Kubernetes, and local development environments. In fact, I accidentally ended up running four Kubernetes clusters at the same time. As a result, my laptop almost gave up and resource usage went through the roof. Thankfully, I learned how to identify and shut down unused clusters, which made my local development environment much more manageable.
Once those issues were resolved, I got back to implementation and started preparing my first GSoC coding-period pull request.
Before pushing the code, I created local unit tests to validate the workflow and ensure the implementation behaved as expected. After successfully running the tests and verifying the results, I pushed the code and opened my PR. I also created a tracking issue to keep track of the remaining work and future tasks.
One thing I am learning every day is that large projects become much easier when you break them into smaller pieces and track progress step by step. What initially looks overwhelming becomes manageable when approached systematically.
Overall, this week was a mix of design, implementation, debugging, testing, and learning. I am continuously growing through the process, and I feel fortunate to experience this kind of engineering and open-source environment.
