## Google Summer of Code Coding Period Update

This week has been one of the best weeks of my GSoC journey so far.
I got three PRs merged, and one of them was particularly special—my KEP (Kubeflow Enhancement Proposal).
Getting the KEP merged is a milestone i am really proud of. It took around 20–25 days of continuous reviews, discussions, and iterations. My mentors told me that many KEPs can take months or even longer to get merged because they define the blueprint of an entire feature.

My perspective as a developer-
Earlier, I always thought writing code was the hardest and most important part of software development. But after working on the KEP, I realized that designing the blueprint is often even more challenging. Every API decision, every workflow, and every design choice affects the implementation that follows. Once the design is solid, writing the code becomes much more structured.
Completing this phase has been one of the biggest learning experiences for me, and I am really grateful to mentors (Andrey Velichkevich, Tariq Hasan, Shekhar Rajak, RISHABH SINGH) for every review comment and every design discussion. Their feedback helped me think beyond just writing code and taught me how to design software that is maintainable and scalable.

Along with the KEP, my SparkClient documentation improvement PR also got merged. In that PR, I improved the documentation and added practical examples to make it easier for new users to understand and start using SparkClient.
With the design phase behind us, I am now focused on implementing FileJob support and the Lifecycle APIs. These features will enable users to submit and manage long-running Spark jobs more easily, making the SparkClient experience better for Kubeflow users.

A funny thing happened this week as well. 😄
I got so immersed in work that I completely lost track of the days. On the day of our mentor meeting, I somehow convinced myself it was Sunday instead of Wednesday. I almost missed the meeting, but thankfully I joined just in time! Definitely setting a few more reminders from now on.
Overall, this week reminded me that engineering isn't just about writing code. Good design, thoughtful reviews, documentation, collaboration, and continuous learning are equally important parts of building software.
Still learning.
Still growing.
On to the next milestone.
